Dealing With Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Bothell

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. How bad it gets depends on placement, whether that is the garage, a closet in the hall or an attic sitting above finished rooms, which is the worst.

Our Bothell team extracts the water, then looks at what surrounded the heater. Closet walls and the platform under the tank are usually soaked at the bottom. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. We measure each area and dry it with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut the Power or GasElectric units get shut down at the breaker. On a gas unit, rotate the gas control to its off setting. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Fabric and cardboard absorb water fast, then keep it pressed to the concrete and walls.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • A temperature and pressure relief valve that opens or fails and discharges water.
  • Anode rods that are used up, leaving the steel inside the tank open to rust.
  • Years of sediment buildup and corrosion wearing a hole in the base of an old steel tank.
  • Excess pressure in the tank from a failed thermostat or a missing expansion tank.
  • Drain valves that crack, work loose or stop sealing completely.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Shared Garage Wall

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Adjacent Room Flooring

Water leaving a heater closet runs under the door and into carpet, laminate or hardwood in the hall. Hot water speeds up swelling in wood flooring and loosens carpet backing.

Stand and Subfloor Damage

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. Rot can weaken it, a real concern once a full new tank sits on top.

Ceilings Under an Attic Heater

Water from an attic unit runs down through insulation to the ceiling beneath it. Soaked insulation loses its insulating value and gets heavy enough to collapse the drywall ceiling.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Remove Standing Water

We remove standing water from the floor, closet or garage and, for attic units, from the overflow pan and ceiling cavity, so the surrounding materials stop absorbing it.

2

Check Around the Heater

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check the platform, wall bases, adjoining rooms and any ceiling below to find the full extent of the wet area.

3

Take Out What Will Not Dry

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. In attics we clear wet insulation so the ceiling joists and drywall below can be dried.

4

Drying and Moisture Readings

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. Moisture readings are recorded, and you hear from us once the space can take the new heater.
